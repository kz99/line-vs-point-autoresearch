0003: ## Abstract
0004: 
0005: Fix a prime $p$, dimension $m=2$, and an integer $100<d<p$. I prove a prime-field refinement of the algebraic cleanup following weighted interpolation. Moreover, a weighted resultant calculation bounds the number of line-polynomial graphs on which both $B$ and $B_Z$ vanish identically by
0006:

0009: $$

For each $L\in\mathcal T$, specialization to $\mathbb F_p(t)$ gives the common root $q_L(t)$, so the resultant vanishes identically on the base line $L$. Its affine-linear equation therefore divides the resultant. Distinct lines give distinct linear factors, proving Theorem 1. If, say, $s=0$, then $V(L(t))\equiv0$ directly and $|\mathcal T|\le\deg V\le v\le uv/d$, since $r>0$ implies $u\ge d$.
0181: 
0182: **[P3 — proved: exact interpolation.]** For an unknown polynomial supported on the $N_d(D)$ weighted monomials, one graph-line identity has degree at most $D$ in $t$ and imposes at most $D+1$ homogeneous linear equations. The strict dimension inequality therefore supplies a nonzero $A$. If $A$ were independent of $Z$, it would vanish identically on $n>D$ base lines; their distinct linear equations would all divide a bivariate polynomial of degree at most $D$, an impossibility. Thus $A$ depends on $Z$. Since $D<p$, every positive $Z$-exponent is nonzero modulo $p$, so $A_Z\ne0$.
0183: 
0184: **[P4 — proved: squarefree primitive reduction.]** Choose $A$ of minimum weighted degree among nonzero graph interpolants. If $A=P^eR$ with $e\ge2$ and $P\nmid R$, then on each graph the product $P^eR$ is zero in the domain $\mathbb F_p[t]$. Hence $PR$ is also zero there, but has smaller weighted degree and still depends on $Z$, a contradiction. Thus $A$ is squarefree. Dividing its coefficient content $H(X,Y)$ makes $B=A/H$ primitive in $Z$ and preserves squarefreeness. On any line not annihilated by $H$, the identity $A(L,P_L)=0$ implies $B(L,P_L)=0$ in the same domain.
0185: 
0186: **[P5 — proved: vertical separability.]** Factor $B$ into distinct irreducibles. If an irreducible factor $C$ divided both $B$ and $B_Z$, squarefreeness would force $C_Z=0$. But $\deg_ZC\le\deg_ZB\le D/d<p$. In characteristic $p$, a polynomial of $Z$-degree below $p$ with zero $Z$-derivative is independent of $Z$. Such a factor would divide every coefficient of $B$, contradicting primitivity. Therefore $\gcd(B,B_Z)=1$. Absolute irreducibility is neither assumed nor needed.

0188: **[P6 — proved: ramification count and incidence loss.]** Put $\delta=\operatorname{wdeg}B$ and $r=\deg_ZB$. Then $r<p$,
0189: 
0190: $$
0191: \operatorname{wdeg}B_Z\le\delta-d,
0192: \qquad
0193: \deg_ZB_Z=r-1.
0194: $$
0195: 
0196: Theorem 1 applied to $B,B_Z$ bounds identically ramified graph lines by
0197:

0199: \Delta=(2r-1)\delta-dr^2
0200: =\frac{\delta^2}{d}-\delta-
0201: rac{(\delta-dr)^2}{d}
0202: \le\frac{\delta^2}{d}-\delta.
0203: $$
0204: 
0205: Let $a=\operatorname{wdeg}A$ and $h=\deg H$, so $a=h+\delta\le D$. Adding the at most $h$ content-trivial lines gives
0206: 
0207: $$
0208: h+\Delta
0209: \le a-\delta+\frac{\delta^2}{d}-\delta
0210: \le\frac{a^2}{d}-a
0211: \le\frac{D^2}{d}-D.
0212: $$
0213: 
0214: The middle inequality follows because
0215: 
0216: $$
0217: \frac{a^2}{d}-a-
0218: \left(a+\frac{\delta^2}{d}-2\delta\right)
0219: =(a-\delta)\left(\frac{a+\delta}{d}-2\right)\ge0.
0220: $$
0221: 
0222: On any other graph, $B_Z(L(t),P_L(t))$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $\delta-d\le D-d$, hence has at most $D-d$ roots. Exceptional lines contribute at most $p$ incidences apiece. Dividing by the total $p^2(p+1)$ incidences proves the claimed loss.

0224: **[P7 — proved: component-sensitive simple-root lift.]** At a point with $B_Z(b,\alpha)\ne0$, exactly one irreducible factor $C$ of $B$ vanishes, and $C_Z(b,\alpha)\ne0$. On every supplied graph through the point, the domain property forces this same factor, rather than another factor, to vanish identically. Newton recursion produces the unique total-degree-at-most-$d$ truncation $Q$ through $\alpha$; it divides only by $C_Z(b,\alpha)$. Its restriction equals every supplied $P_L$. Then $C(X,Y,Q)$ has degree at most $\delta_C$ and vanishes on more than $\delta_C$ distinct lines, so it is zero. The kernel of substitution $Z\mapsto Q$ is $(Z-Q)$; irreducibility forces $C$ to be associated to $Z-Q$.

0316: \quad

0324: \iff
0325: \varepsilon^3\gtrsim\frac dp.
0326: $$

0329: 
0330: $$
0331: \frac{D^2/d}{p^2}=O\left(\frac1{\varepsilon p}\right),
0332: $$
0333: 
0334: where the ordinary-degree estimate was $D^2/p^2=O(d/(\varepsilon p))$. This factor-$d$ improvement is not controlling.

0336: No Markov loss occurs in the new proof. KTZ Lemma 3.2 uses a Chernoff estimate and a union bound over at most $p(p+1)$ lines, followed by Chebyshev for $|\mathcal R|$; these introduce constants and the requirement $k_0\gtrsim\log p$, not another power of $\varepsilon$. KTZ Lemma 2.1 is proved by Cauchy–Schwarz and contributes $1/\sqrt{p+1}$; Lemma 4.1 explicitly budgets it as at most $\varepsilon/8000$. 0338: ## Counterexample Attempts
0339: 
0340: 1. **Adversarial random table:** [P11] rigorously proves the linear-scale floor and rules out exponents greater than $1$. It does not refute the campaign target.
0341: 
0342: 2. **Higher-Hasse lifting:** [P10] refutes the tempting replacement of simple roots by finite Hasse multiplicity. The failure already occurs at the campaign's lower boundary $d=101$.
0343: 
0344: 3. **Concentrated good directions:** choose $d+1$ distinct directions $u_i$ and linear forms $\ell_i$ vanishing on them, and set
0345: 
0346:    $$f(X,Y)=\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}\ell_i(X,Y).$$
0347: 
0348:    On every affine line in direction $u_i$, the leading coefficient vanishes, so the restriction has degree at most $d$. Use that restriction as $P_L$ and use $P_L=0$ in all other directions. The exact local agreement is
0349: 
0350:    $$
0351:    \frac{(d+1)p^2+(p-d)((p-1)(d+1)+1)}{p^2(p+1)},
0352:    $$
0353: 
0354:    while $Q=0$ has global agreement
0355: 
0356:    $$
0357:    \frac{1+(d+1)(p-1)}{p^2}.
0358:    $$
0359: 
0360:    Both are $\Theta(d/p)$ when $d=o(p)$. Thus good directions may be concentrated at the conjectured linear scale, but the example still has the required order of global agreement.
0361: 
0362: 4. **Inseparability:** $Z^p-X$ is primitive, irreducible, and squarefree as a multivariate polynomial, yet its ordinary $Z$-derivative is zero. Its weighted degree is at least $pd$, so it is excluded precisely by $D<p$. This confirms that the auxiliary degree condition is doing real characteristic work.
0363: 
0364: 5. **Near-$p$ regime:** if $d>p/2$ and $D<p$, then $\deg_ZB=1$. For $d=p-1$, $D=d$ and $B_Z$ is constant. No inseparability obstruction appears; instead, the KTZ cubic hypothesis with a large absolute constant may simply be empty when $d/p$ is too close to one.

0366: ## Characteristic Audit
0367: 
0368: - All rings used for factorization are formal polynomial UFDs. Polynomial functions are never substituted for formal polynomials.
0369: - The only characteristic-sensitive implication is $C_Z=0$ and $\deg_ZC<p$ implies $C$ is independent of $Z$.
0370: - Primitivity, not absolute irreducibility, excludes such a factor.
0371: - The resultant is not divided by a leading coefficient, so fibers where $\deg_ZB$ drops are included in its zero set.
0372: - The simple-root Newton recursion divides only by the nonzero value $B_Z(b,\alpha)$.
0373: - Hasse multiplicity in [P9] is defined through maximal-ideal powers, so no factorial denominators occur.
0374: - The substitution of a multiplicity-$s$ lifted point into a line graph produces a root of multiplicity at least $s$ because the lifted maximal ideal maps into $(t-t_0)$.
0375: - The $p=409$ pencil confirms that higher Hasse order does not give uniqueness.
0376: - At $p=103,d=101$, the exact dimension check shows failure of the raw interpolation inequalities, not failure of the theorem or a small-degree obstruction.

0378: ## Limitations
0379: 
0380: The result improves algebraic factor control but not the degree $D$ of the explainer. It therefore cannot by itself beat exponent $1/3$. The main surviving bottleneck is the need to spend up to $D-d$ bad roots on every nonexceptional line, together with the earlier need for more than $D$ accepted crossings to propagate the line identity.
0381: 
0382: The automatic vertical-separability argument requires the auxiliary interpolant degree $D<p$. It does not address a hypothetical construction with $D\ge p$, and the example $Z^p-X$ shows why that extension is nonformal.
0383: 
0384: The multiplicity obstruction covers only uniform ambient multiplicity certified by the raw number of jet equations. It leaves open dependent node conditions, nonuniform multiplicity, component-specific interpolation, and directional jets.
0385: 
0386: The component-sensitive corollary helps only if one proves that the component supporting many accepted line graphs has weighted degree substantially below $D$; no such distribution theorem is proved here.
0387: 
0388: Finally, finite-field arithmetic checks are used only for falsification and endpoint auditing. They do not establish the asymptotic soundness statement.
