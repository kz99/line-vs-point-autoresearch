0001: # Popularity-sensitive core decoding and a bounded-star-energy obstruction
0002: 
0003: ## Abstract
0004: 
0005: Fix a prime field $F_p$, dimension $m=2$, and an integer $100<d<p$. I prove an asymmetric refinement of the accepted-component decoder. If an accepted subgraph has point minimum degree $u$, line minimum degree $v$, line measure $τ$, and $w=min(u,v)$, then the two conditions
0006: 
0007: $$
0008: uv \ge 2^{60}dp,
0009: \qquad
0010: uw^2 \ge 2^{60}dτp^2
0011: $$
0012: 
0013: force one total-degree-at-most-$d$ polynomial to agree with the point table on at least $v/(800p)$ of all points.
0014: 
0015: For the original accepted graph, let $u_*$ be the largest point-degree threshold supporting accepted-incidence mass at least $ε/2$. The component theorem implies
0016: 
0017: $$
0018: u_*ε^2 \ge 2^{70}d
0019: \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0020: agr(f,Q) \ge ε/12800.
0021: $$
0022: 
0023: Thus a hypothesis $u_*\ge κ ε^a p$ gives the conditional threshold $ε\ge(2^{70}/κ)^{1/(a+2)}(d/p)^{1/(a+2)}$. This interpolates from exponent $1/3$ at the universally available scale $u_*=Ω(εp)$ to exponent $1/2$ when $u_*=Ω(p)$. It does not improve the unconditional benchmark.
0024: 
0025: I also prove a template-popularity lemma and a complementary obstruction: for every fixed star order, diffuse modal-constant tables can have star energy only $O(ε^s)$ while every degree-101 global polynomial has agreement $o(ε)$. Consequently, a scale-free bounded-order collision-energy increment cannot supply the missing popularity.

0027: ## Test and Notation
0028: 
0029: There are $p^2$ points and $p(p+1)$ affine lines. Every point lies on $p+1$ lines and every line contains $p$ points. Each affine line $L$ carries a univariate polynomial $P_L$ of degree at most the ambient integer $d$, and $f:F_p^2\to F_p$ is the point table. The sampling rule is exactly a uniformly random affine line followed by a uniformly random point on it; equivalently, it is a uniform affine incidence. Put
0030: 
0031: $$
0032: ε=Pr_{L,\,x\in L}[P_L(x)=f(x)].
0033: $$
0034: 
0035: The accepted graph has edge $(x,L)$ exactly when $x\in L$ and $P_L(x)=f(x)$. All global polynomials below have total degree at most $d$.
0036: 
0037: For a subgraph $G=(X,T,E)$, let
0038: 
0039: $$
0040: t=|T|,
0041: \qquad
0042: τ=\frac{t}{p(p+1)},
0043: $$
0044: 
0045: and let $u$ and $v$ denote its point-side and line-side minimum degrees, respectively. For the full accepted graph, write $a_x$ for the accepted degree of $x$ and define the absolute upper-tail incidence mass
0046: 
0047: $$
0048: δ_j=\frac{1}{p^2(p+1)}\sum_{x:a_x\ge j}a_x,
0049: \qquad
0050: u_*=max\{j\in\{1,\ldots,p+1\}:δ_j\ge ε/2\}.
0051: $$
0052: 
0053: When $ε>0$, this set is nonempty. Since points below $ε(p+1)/2$ carry less than half of all accepted edges,
0054: 
0055: $$
0056: u_*\ge \left\lceil\frac{ε(p+1)}2\right\rceil.
0057: $$
0058: 
0059: For $s\ge2$, $Γ_s$ denotes the probability that $s$ ordered distinct uniformly sampled lines through a uniform point all accept there.

0071: ## Theorem
0072: 
0073: **Theorem A (proved asymmetric core decoder).** Let $p$ be prime, let $100<d<p$, and let $G=(X,T,E)$ be a nonempty accepted subgraph. Suppose every $x\in X$ has degree at least the integer $u$, every $L\in T$ has degree at least the integer $v$, $τ=|T|/[p(p+1)]$, and $w=min(u,v)$. If
0074: 
0075: $$
0076: uv\ge2^{60}dp,
0077: \qquad
0078: uw^2\ge2^{60}dτp^2,
0079: $$
0080: 
0081: then there is a single $Q\in F_p[X,Y]$ of total degree at most $d$ satisfying
0082: 
0083: $$
0084: Pr_x[Q(x)=f(x)]\ge\frac{v}{800p}.
0085: $$
0086: 
0087: Connectedness of the input is not required.
0088: 
0089: **Corollary B (proved implication; conditional exponent).** With $u_*$ defined above, if
0090: 
0091: $$
0092: u_*ε^2\ge2^{70}d,
0093: $$
0094: 
0095: then some total-degree-at-most-$d$ polynomial satisfies $agr(f,Q)\ge ε/12800$. More generally, if $0\le a\le1$, $0<κ\le1$, and $u_*\ge κ ε^a p$, then the conclusion holds whenever
0096: 
0097: $$
0098: ε\ge\left(\frac{2^{70}}κ\right)^{1/(a+2)}
0099:        \left(\frac dp\right)^{1/(a+2)}.
0100: $$
0101: 
0102: The exponent improvement for $a<1$ is conditional because no such popularity tail is known for every table.
0103: 
0104: **Lemma C (proved template popularity).** Let $T_1,\ldots,T_R$ be disjoint line families and let $Q_1,\ldots,Q_R$ be total-degree-at-most-$d$ polynomials such that $P_L=Q_j|_L$ for $L\in T_j$. Let $τ_j=|T_j|/[p(p+1)]$, $τ=\sum_jτ_j$, let $δ>0$ be the normalized accepted-incidence mass on their union, and put $α=max_j agr(f,Q_j)$. Then
0105: 
0106: $$
0107: δ\le ατ+\sqrt{\frac{Rατ}{p+1}}.
0108: $$
0109: 
0110: In particular, $R\leδ(p+1)$ implies $α\geδ/4$.
0111: 
0112: **Proposition D (proved bounded-star-energy obstruction).** Fix $S\ge2$. For all sufficiently large primes $p$, at the legal ambient degree $d=101$ there is a deterministic table using constant line polynomials such that, for
0113: 
0114: $$
0115: r=\left\lfloor\frac{\log p}{4\log\log p}\right\rfloor,
0116: $$
0117: 
0118: one has
0119: 
0120: $$
0121: \frac{r}{130p}\le ε\le\frac{26r}{p},
0122: \qquad
0123: max_{deg Q\le101}agr(f,Q)<\frac2p,
0124: $$
0125: 
0126: and, simultaneously for $2\le s\le S$,
0127: 
0128: $$
0129: Γ_s\le2^{s+2}(S-1)ε^s.
0130: $$
0131: 
0132: A fully explicit sufficient meaning of sufficiently large is obtained by setting $u_0=\lceil4\log p/\log\log p\rceil$ and $v_0=\lceil8\log p/\log\log p\rceil$ and requiring
0133: 
0134: $$
0135: r\ge133,
0136: \quad r^2\le p,
0137: \quad p\ge8r!,
0138: \quad u_0+1\le17r,
0139: \quad v_0\le33r,
0140: $$
0141: 
0142: $$
0143: \frac{2p}{(u_0+1)!}\le1,
0144: \quad
0145: \frac{2p^3}{v_0!}\le\frac18,
0146: \quad
0147: p^{5253}(e/4)^p\le\frac18,
0148: \quad
0149: S\le p+1.

0154: **[P1] (proved: asymmetric mixing).** Put $ξ=|X|/p^2$, $ρ=|E|/[p^2(p+1)]$, $α_0=u/(p+1)$, $β_0=v/p$, and $λ=(p+1)^{-1/2}$. Minimum degree and KTZ Lemma 2.1 give
0155: 
0156: $$
0157: α_0ξ\leρ,
0158: \qquad
0159: β_0τ\leρ,
0160: \qquad
0161: ρ\leξτ+λ\sqrt{ξτ}.
0162: $$
0163: 
0164: If $τ<α_0/4$, the first and third inequalities imply $ξ<(16/9)λ^2τ/α_0^2$. Substitution into the third inequality and comparison with $β_0τ\leρ$ give $α_0β_0<2λ^2$, or $uv<2p$. This contradicts the first hypothesis. The symmetric calculation gives
0165: 
0166: $$
0167: τ\ge\frac{u}{4(p+1)},
0168: \qquad
0169: ξ\ge\frac{v}{4p}.
0170: $$
0171: 
0172: Thus $t\ge up/4$. Substituting the line-measure bound into the second hypothesis yields
0173: 
0174: $$
0175: w^2\ge\frac{2^{60}dp^2}{4(p+1)}\ge2^{57}dp.
0176: $$
0177: 
0178: This last inequality, rather than an unstated fixed-$d$ assumption, supplies all logarithmic room below.
0179: 
0180: **[P2] (proved: selector and probability losses).** The first hypothesis and $v\le p$ give $u>8$. Select every line of $T$ independently with probability $8/u$. A point accepted on a target line $L$ is called reached only through a selected line in $R$ other than $L$. It has at least $u-1$ eligible lines, so its reach probability exceeds $3/4$. For distinct points of $L$, the eligible line sets are disjoint because two distinct points determine their unique common line, which is the excluded line $L$. Chernoff therefore bounds the probability that at most half of $N_G(L)$ is reached by $e^{-v/24}$.
0181: 
0182: Since $v\ge w\ge\sqrt{2^{57}dp}$, $2p^2e^{-v/24}<1/4$; this accounts explicitly for the union bound over all target lines. The sample-size mean is $8t/u\ge2p$. Its lower- and upper-tail Chernoff bounds are respectively at most $e^{-p/4}$ and $e^{-2p/3}$. Hence some choice has
0183: 
0184: $$
0185: \frac{4t}{u}\le r=|R|\le\frac{16t}{u}
0186: $$
0187: 
0188: and reaches more than half of every line's accepted points. In particular, $r\ge p$.

0190: **[P3] (proved: interpolation).** Set
0191: 
0192: $$
0193: D=16(\lfloor\sqrt{dr}\rfloor+1).
0194: $$
0195: 
0196: Then $D^2>256dr$ and
0197: 
0198: $$
0199: D^2\le1024dr
0200:  \le2^{14}\frac{dt}{u}
0201:  \le2^{15}\frac{dτp^2}{u}
0202:  \le2^{-45}w^2.
0203: $$
0204: 
0205: Thus $D<w/5000$, $D<p$, and $r\ge p>D$. KTZ Lemma 2.2 gives $dim V_D\ge D^3/(64d)>r(D+1)$. Each formal identity on a selected line costs at most $D+1$ homogeneous conditions, so a nonzero candidate in $V_D$ exists. If it were Z-independent, KTZ Lemma 2.5 and $r>D$ would force it to vanish identically. Its allowed positive Z-exponents are not divisible by $p$, so its Z-derivative is nonzero.
0206: 
0207: Let $S_R$ be the finite union of the selected polynomial graphs and choose a nonzero polynomial $A$ of minimum weighted degree among polynomials vanishing on $S_R$ with $A_Z\ne0$. KTZ Lemma 2.3 makes $A$ squarefree. Its weight is at most $D<p$, so pointwise vanishing at all $p$ parameters on a selected line is a formal identity. This also explains why repeated-factor removal cannot lose the line identities.
0208: 
0209: **[P4] (proved: identity extension).** Every $L\in T$ has more than $v/2>D$ accepted points reached through a selected $M\ne L$. At such a point,
0210: 
0211: $$
0212: P_L(x)=f(x)=P_M(x),
0213: $$
0214: 
0215: so $A(x,P_L(x))=0$. The restricted polynomial $A(L(t),P_L(t))$ has degree at most $D$ and more than $D$ roots; hence it vanishes formally. There is no Markov or multiplicity loss at this step.
0216: 
0217: **[P5] (proved: prime-field simple-root cleanup).** Write $A=HB$, where $H(X,Y)$ is the gcd of the Z-coefficients of $A$. At most $D$ affine lines are contained in $H=0$. On every other line, division in the integral domain $F_p[t]$ gives $B(L(t),P_L(t))\equiv0$. The polynomial $B$ is squarefree, Z-primitive, Z-dependent, and has degree below $p$.
0218: 
0219: Moreover, $gcd(B,B_Z)=1$. Indeed, if an irreducible $C$ divided both, squarefreeness and reduction of $(CE)_Z$ modulo $C$ would give $C|C_Z$, hence $C_Z=0$. Since $deg_Z C<p$, this makes $C$ Z-independent, contradicting Z-primitivity. KTZ Lemma 2.6 now bounds the lines on whose graphs both $B$ and $B_Z$ vanish by $D(D-1)$. On every other line, $B_Z(L(t),P_L(t))$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $D$, so it has at most $D$ accepted roots. Thus at most
0220: 
0221: $$
0222: pD^2+Dt
0223: $$
0224: 
0225: accepted edges are deleted. No discriminant or derivative is divided by.

0231:  \le\frac Dv+\frac{pD^2}{vt}

0233:  <\frac1{4000}.

0237:

0244: **[P7] (proved: lift, propagate, recover measure).** Every surviving point is incident to an identity line and hence satisfies $B(x,f(x))=0$; retained edges also certify $B_Z(x,f(x))\ne0$. It produces one total-degree-at-most-$d$ polynomial $Q$ with $B(X,Y,Q(X,Y))\equiv0$. The one-variable simple-root uniqueness used in KTZ Lemma 6.3 propagates this same $Q$ through the component.

Peel point degrees below $U=\lfloor u_*/2\rfloor$ and line degrees below $V=\lfloorδ_*p/4\rfloor$. Point deletions cost at most $E_*/2$, and line deletions cost at most

Universally $u_*\geεp/2$, so this recovers exponent $1/3$ with a large constant. For $a<1$ it is a genuine conditional improvement; $a=0$ gives exponent $1/2$.

0286: **[P9] (proved: template popularity).** Put $S_j=\{x:Q_j(x)=f(x)\}$ and $α_j=|S_j|/p^2$. The carried edges in $T_j$ are exactly the incidences between $S_j$ and $T_j$. KTZ Lemma 2.1 gives

0288: $$

0392: ## Counterexample Attempts
0393: 
0394: The modal table is diffuse: every affine line in every direction supports acceptance, no line accepts more than $O(\log p/\log\log p)$ points, and yet every bounded star moment stays at its baseline scale. It rules out energy increments based only on a fixed number of coincident accepted lines.
0395: 
0396: The regular Hall carrier from the active corpus has full line support and pair energy $Θ(ε^2)$ but a perfect constant decoder. Together, the two examples show that low collision energy occurs in both easy and hard tables. An effective dichotomy must inspect algebraic coherence, not merely degrees or support.
0397:
